How to Check if an Image Is Real – for Free

You don't need paid software to investigate a suspicious image. These free tools and techniques, used together, will answer most “is this real?” questions.

1. RealTell – read the hidden labels

RealTell reads Content Credentials, AI metadata and camera data inside the file and gives you a verdict with the evidence behind it. It runs in your browser, so private photos stay on your device. Best for: original files from the creator or sender.

2. Google Lens – find where else it appears

Google Lens searches the web for copies and similar images. It is the quickest way to discover whether a photo has been published before, by whom, and in what context. Best for: images that have spread online.

3. TinEye – find the oldest copy

TinEye is a reverse image search engine that lets you sort results by date, which helps you find the earliest known version of an image. Older copies are often higher quality and more likely to retain metadata.

4. The official Content Credentials verifier

The Content Authenticity Initiative's verifier at contentcredentials.org/verify shows the full details of a Content Credential, including the signer and the edit history. Use it when RealTell finds a credential and you want to inspect it in depth.

5. Your own eyes – zoomed in

Open the image at full size and look closely at hands, text, backgrounds, reflections and shadows. Our guide on how to tell if a photo is AI lists the most useful clues.

A simple routine that works

  1. Check the file in RealTell.
  2. If no label is found, run a reverse image search on Google Lens and TinEye.
  3. Look for the original source and any reliable reporting about it.
  4. Examine the details yourself.
  5. Decide based on all the evidence – and say “unverified” if it's still unclear.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free tool that can always tell if an image is real?

No. Free tools give strong evidence in many cases, but some images can't be verified with certainty.

Do these tools upload my photo?

RealTell does not. Reverse image search engines do upload the image to their servers, so avoid them for private photos.

Can I check images on my phone?

Yes. RealTell, Google Lens and TinEye all work in mobile browsers.
