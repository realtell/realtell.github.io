How to Spot AI-Generated Videos

AI video tools such as Sora, Google Veo, Runway and Kling can now create clips that look like real footage. Use this checklist to judge a suspicious video before you share it.

1. Check the file if you can get it

If you have the original video file, drop it into RealTell. Videos downloaded directly from AI tools that support Content Credentials – including Sora – carry a label saying they were AI-generated. Phone videos often carry camera tags identifying the device.

Videos saved from TikTok, Instagram, YouTube or X have usually been re-encoded and lost this information, so “No AI labels found” is common and not meaningful on its own.

2. Watch for visual glitches

  • Morphing: objects, hands or faces that subtly change shape from one moment to the next.
  • Disappearing details: buttons, jewellery, people in the background or cars that appear and vanish.
  • Unreadable text: signs and labels that are blurry or change as the camera moves.
  • Odd motion: walking that glides, fabric and hair that move strangely, liquids that behave like gel.
  • Eyes and teeth: unnatural blinking, teeth that blur together when someone speaks.

Pause the video and step through it frame by frame – many errors only last a fraction of a second.

3. Listen carefully

If the video includes speech, check whether lip movements match the words exactly, whether the voice sounds flat or overly smooth, and whether background noise matches the scene.

4. Consider the length and style

Many AI clips are short – often well under a minute – and have a polished, cinematic look with smooth camera moves. Real eyewitness footage tends to be shakier and messier.

5. Verify the source

  • Find the earliest upload. Take a screenshot of a key frame and run a reverse image search.
  • Check whether reliable news outlets have reported the same event.
  • Look at the account that posted it first: does it usually post AI content?
  • Read the comments – other viewers often spot problems or find the original.
If a video shows something shocking that would be big news, and no reputable outlet is reporting it, treat it as unverified until proven otherwise.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI video detectors tell for sure?

No tool is perfect. Embedded labels are reliable when present; pixel-based detectors can be wrong in both directions.

Does a visible Sora watermark prove a video is AI?

It is a strong sign, but watermarks can be added or cropped out. Combine it with other checks.

How do I check a video from TikTok?

Labels are usually removed, so focus on visual signs and source checks. Some platforms show their own “AI-generated” labels too.
