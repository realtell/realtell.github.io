How to check a video for Sora

  1. Use the original download from Sora or the person who made it. Screen recordings and re-uploads lose the label.
  2. Drop the video into the checker. Large files are fine – RealTell reads the parts of the file where metadata is stored.
  3. Look at the verdict. “AI-generated” with Sora named as the source means the label is present.

How Sora videos are labelled

OpenAI attaches Content Credentials (C2PA) to Sora videos. The label is stored in the video file and records that the content was generated by AI. Videos shared in Sora's own app may also show a visible watermark, but visible watermarks are easy to crop out – the embedded label is a more dependable signal while it survives.

Why most viral AI videos show “No AI labels found”

By the time an AI video goes viral, it has usually been downloaded, re-encoded and re-uploaded several times. TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, X and messaging apps typically rebuild the file and drop its metadata along the way. If RealTell reports that the video was re-encoded and finds no labels, that is normal for shared videos – it doesn't mean the video is real.

Signs a video may be AI-generated

  • Objects or people that appear, vanish or change shape between frames.
  • Hands, teeth and fingers that blur or morph during movement.
  • Text in the scene that is unreadable or changes as the camera moves.
  • Physics that feels slightly off – liquids, fabric, hair or crowds moving unnaturally.
  • Very short clips (often under 20 seconds) with a cinematic, overly perfect look.

Frequently asked questions

Can RealTell detect Sora videos downloaded from TikTok or Instagram?

Usually not. These platforms re-encode videos and remove most metadata, including Content Credentials.

What about Google Veo, Runway or Kling videos?

RealTell checks every video for Content Credentials and AI tool names, so any generator that embeds a label will be detected. Support varies by tool.

Is there a size limit?

No hard limit. For very large videos RealTell reads the start and end of the file, where metadata is normally stored.
