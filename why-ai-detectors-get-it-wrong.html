Why AI Image Detectors Get It Wrong

Many online “AI detectors” give a confident percentage – “97% AI” – for any image you upload. Independent tests keep showing that these numbers can be badly wrong. Here's why, and how to use detection tools without hurting innocent people.

Two very different kinds of detector

Pixel-based detectors use a machine-learning model to look at the image itself and guess whether it “looks like” AI. Provenance checkers, like RealTell, read information stored in the file – such as Content Credentials – that the creating tool or camera put there.

The first kind always gives an answer, even when it is guessing. The second kind only answers when there is real evidence, and says so when there isn't.

Why pixel-based detectors make mistakes

  • Ordinary processing looks “artificial”. Compression, resizing, noise reduction, beauty filters, HDR and upscaling can all create patterns that models associate with AI.
  • New generators beat old training data. A detector trained on last year's AI images can miss images from newer models – or mistake their quirks for real photography.
  • Style isn't evidence. Highly polished photography, digital art and illustration are often flagged simply because they look clean and stylised.
  • Percentages sound more precise than they are. A “90% AI” score is not a 90% chance the image is fake; it is a model's confidence on data that may not match yours.

Published tests of popular detectors have repeatedly found that some flag a significant share of genuine photos as AI-generated. For photographers, artists, students and job applicants, a false accusation can have real consequences.

How to use detectors responsibly

  1. Prefer evidence over scores. A signed Content Credential that says “created by AI” is far stronger than any percentage.
  2. Treat “not detected” and “detected” as starting points. Follow up with a reverse image search and source checks.
  3. Use more than one method. Agreement between independent methods is more meaningful than a single tool.
  4. Ask for the original. Photographers can usually provide the RAW file or other shots from the same session.
  5. Never accuse someone based on one tool. Explain what evidence you have, and give them the chance to respond.

Frequently asked questions

Are any AI detectors 100% accurate?

No. Every method has limits. Signed provenance labels are the most reliable evidence when they are present.

My real photo was flagged as AI. What can I do?

Keep your original files, RAW images and nearby shots from the same session. If your camera supports Content Credentials, turn them on for future photos.

Why doesn't RealTell give a percentage?

Because a percentage implies certainty we don't have. We show the evidence found and how strong it is instead.
