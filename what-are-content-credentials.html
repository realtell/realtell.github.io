What Are Content Credentials (C2PA)? A Plain-English Guide

Content Credentials are like a nutrition label for digital media. They travel inside a photo or video and record where it came from – including whether AI was involved. Here's how they work and how to check them.

The short version

Content Credentials are a standard way of attaching provenance information – “where did this come from and what happened to it?” – to an image, video or audio file. They are based on an open technical standard created by the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA), whose members include Adobe, Microsoft, Google, OpenAI, camera makers and news organisations.

What a Content Credential contains

A credential (technically called a manifest) can record:

  • the tool or device that created the file – for example an AI generator or a specific camera;
  • whether the content was captured, generated by AI or edited with AI;
  • edits made afterwards, and which earlier files were combined;
  • a digital signature from the organisation that issued it.

The signature is the important part. If someone changes the image or the label after it was signed, the signature no longer matches, so the tampering can be detected.

Who uses Content Credentials?

  • AI generators: OpenAI (ChatGPT images and Sora), Adobe Firefly and Google's Imagen/Gemini add credentials to the content they generate.
  • Creative software: Adobe Photoshop and Lightroom can attach credentials, including when AI features such as Generative Fill are used.
  • Cameras and phones: a growing number of professional cameras and some smartphones can sign photos at the moment of capture.
  • Platforms: some social networks and search engines read credentials to show an “AI info” or “Made with AI” label.

Notably, not every AI tool participates. Midjourney, for example, does not add signed Content Credentials.

Why credentials go missing

Credentials are stored in the file's metadata. Anything that rebuilds the file without copying that metadata will remove them: screenshots, many messaging apps, most social media uploads, and some editing or conversion tools. That's why a missing credential tells you very little about whether a file is real.

How to check Content Credentials

  1. Drop the file into RealTell for an instant, private summary of what the credential says.
  2. For a full technical inspection of the signature and edit history, use the official verifier at contentcredentials.org/verify (linked on every RealTell result).

Why this matters

As AI-generated media becomes harder to recognise by eye, signed provenance information is becoming one of the most dependable ways to know what you are looking at. Regulations in several regions, including the EU AI Act, are also pushing for AI-generated content to be clearly labelled – and Content Credentials are a leading way to do that.

Frequently asked questions

Is C2PA the same as a watermark?

No. A C2PA credential is signed metadata stored in the file. Invisible watermarks, such as Google's SynthID, are hidden in the pixels themselves. Some companies use both.

Can Content Credentials be faked?

The signature makes forging a valid credential very difficult, but metadata can still be removed. That's why missing credentials don't prove anything.

Do I need special software to see them?

No. RealTell shows them in your browser, and the official verifier offers a detailed view.
