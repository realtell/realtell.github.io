How Google marks AI images

Google adds Content Credentials (C2PA) to images generated with its Imagen models, including images made in the Gemini app. Many Google AI images also carry an IPTC “Digital Source Type” tag that marks them as created by a trained algorithm. RealTell reads both.

Google additionally uses SynthID, an invisible watermark woven into the pixels. SynthID can survive screenshots and light edits, but it can only be read with Google's own tools. If RealTell finds no label on an image you suspect came from Google, checking it with Google's SynthID tools is a good next step.

Gemini edits vs. Gemini creations

Not every Google AI label means the whole picture is fake. Phone features such as Magic Editor change parts of real photos. When the label says content was edited or combined with AI, RealTell shows “AI-edited” rather than “AI-generated”, so you can tell a retouched holiday photo from an image created from scratch.

Tips for accurate results

  • Check the file as downloaded from Gemini, Google Photos or the sender – not a screenshot.
  • Files shared through chat apps and social networks usually lose their labels.
  • If you see “Likely real” with camera data from a Pixel phone, the photo was taken with the camera; check the evidence list for any AI-edit labels as well.

Frequently asked questions

Does RealTell read SynthID?

No. SynthID is an invisible watermark that only Google's tools can read. RealTell reads Content Credentials and metadata stored in the file.

Will Gemini images always be detected?

Only while their labels are intact. Screenshots and re-uploads usually remove them.

Is it free?

Yes – free, unlimited and private.
