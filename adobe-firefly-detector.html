How Adobe labels AI content

Adobe was one of the founders of the Content Authenticity Initiative, and Firefly adds Content Credentials to the images it generates. The same applies to AI features inside Adobe apps, such as Generative Fill and Generative Expand in Photoshop.

AI-generated vs. AI-edited

RealTell separates two very different situations:

  • AI-generated – the whole image was created by Firefly from a text prompt.
  • AI-edited – a real photo where part of the picture was added, removed or extended with Generative Fill. The rest of the image may be a genuine photograph.

This distinction matters. A product photo with a cleaned-up background is very different from a completely invented scene, and the evidence list on your result shows which one the label describes.

When the label disappears

Adobe preserves Content Credentials through its own editing pipeline, but other apps may not. Exporting through a non-supporting program, screenshotting, or posting to most social networks can remove the label. If that happens, RealTell will show “No AI labels found”.

Frequently asked questions

Can RealTell show which part of the photo was edited?

No. It reads what the label says about the file as a whole. The official Content Credentials verifier can show more detail about the edit history.

Does Lightroom AI editing show up?

If the export includes Content Credentials that declare AI use, RealTell will report it. Ordinary exports without credentials will show camera data only.

Is the check private?

Yes. Files are analysed inside your browser and never uploaded.
