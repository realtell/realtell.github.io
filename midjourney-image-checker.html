Why Midjourney images are harder to detect

Unlike ChatGPT, Adobe Firefly and Google Gemini, Midjourney does not attach signed Content Credentials to its images. Some Midjourney downloads carry an unsigned IPTC metadata tag identifying them as AI-generated, or text mentioning Midjourney. When RealTell finds those, it reports “Likely AI-generated”.

Because these tags are unsigned and are removed by most apps, many Midjourney images you find online will carry no label at all. For those, the honest result is “No AI labels found” – which does not mean the image is real.

How to judge a suspected Midjourney image

  1. Reverse image search it with Google Lens or TinEye. Midjourney images are often posted first in AI art communities or on Midjourney's own gallery pages.
  2. Look for the Midjourney style. Dramatic cinematic lighting, extremely polished skin, painterly depth of field and a “too perfect” composition are common.
  3. Zoom into details. Jewellery, fingers, background text, fences, stair railings and reflections often contain small errors.
  4. Check the aspect ratio. Midjourney frequently outputs sizes like 1456 × 816 or 816 × 1456 – a hint, not proof.
Pixel-based “AI detectors” that claim to recognise Midjourney can be useful as a second opinion, but they regularly mislabel real photos too. Read why AI detectors get it wrong before relying on one.

Frequently asked questions

Does Midjourney add a watermark?

Midjourney does not add Content Credentials. Some downloads include an unsigned metadata tag, which RealTell will detect if it is still present.

Why did my Midjourney image show “No AI labels found”?

Its metadata was missing – either Midjourney didn't include a tag or it was removed when the file was shared or edited.

What is the most reliable way to check?

Combine a metadata check, a reverse image search and a careful look at the details. No single method is perfect.
